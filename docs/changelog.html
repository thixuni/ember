Changelog

Every version, and what changed in it.

Installed copies update themselves, so you will usually already have the top of this list. Each version number here is one you can still download from the releases page.

1.3.0 Sep 29, 2026 Latest
  • Workspaces. The planner divides into as many as six separate planners — Personal, Studio, whatever you need — each with its own categories, board lanes, tasks, routines, notes, documents and tracked time. Switch and the whole sidebar changes with you.
  • An All workspaces view, for when you do want to see the lot at once.
  • The dashboard always shows every workspace, and says so, because today does not belong to one part of your life.
  • Categories belong to a workspace and are edited where the workspaces are, in Settings, under the workspace they belong to, under the one they belong to.
  • A task moves workspace from its panel, taking its lane, its category and its subtasks with it.
  • The sidebar is one row at the top — the mark, then the workspace switcher — and names what the pointer is on when it is folded to its icons.
  • The dashboard reads as cards rather than ruled columns, with its five sections in five colours.
  • Coloured stripes down the left edge of things are gone from every screen.
  • A planner from 1.2.3 carries over whole: its boards become workspaces and nothing moves.
1.2.3 Sep 29, 2026
  • Drag a block on the calendar to another day or hour. Tasks and blocked-out time move outright; a routine asks whether you meant this day only, this day onward, or the whole routine.
  • The dashboard became three columns that each scroll inside themselves, so a long schedule can no longer push what is overdue off the bottom of the page.
  • Quiet hours, a night at a time. Each night of the week keeps its own window, so weekends can start later than Tuesdays.
  • A missed routine day can be let off, so a week away does not end a streak.
  • More than one board, each with its own lanes — later replaced by workspaces in 1.3.0.
  • Walk back through earlier weeks on a routine’s card, and a cleaner streak log.
  • Enter saves a subtask and opens the next one.
  • The task panel gained a picker for which board a task is on, beside its lane.
  • The Notes guided tour’s first tip came back after the button it pointed at was removed.
1.2.2 Sep 29, 2026
  • Streaks with no ceiling. The whole history counts rather than the last year, so a year-old run is no longer capped and a routine due twice a year can still be made up.
  • A streak log: the year drawn as twelve calendars, every square a day you can still check off, over four numbers — current run, best ever, times kept, and how many of the days it was due.
  • A streak counts times kept rather than days, so a weekly review reads “28 Fridays”.
1.2.1 Sep 24, 2026
  • Light is the default, and only you change it — the planner no longer follows the computer unless you ask it to.
  • Backups say whether they worked. A folder that had been moved, renamed or was on a drive that was not plugged in used to fail in silence and still report success.
  • Settings › Account › Run setup again, without throwing anything away.
  • Setup’s categories step became chips with the colour and icon opening over the page.
  • One logo everywhere, and it stands still.
1.2.0 Sep 23, 2026
  • The planner is called Ember. A flame for a mark, a warm accent, and a wordmark of its own.
  • Setup is a page of its own rather than a dialog, lit by one fire that burns through every step with a live picture of whatever you are answering beside it.
1.1.2 Sep 23, 2026
  • A task has one date. The second deadline that sat under it was taken out — people read the two as two deadlines and the wrong one was always the one being looked at.
  • Right-click menus on everything: a task anywhere on the page, a routine, a note, a category, a board lane, blocked-out time.
  • Guided tips, one short tour per screen, played the first time you open it.
  • A folding sidebar, and a list view you can group by date, lane, category, priority or a field of your own.
  • Each board lane scrolls its own cards, so every lane’s heading stays put.
  • US English throughout.
1.1.1 Sep 23, 2026
  • Drag down an empty stretch of the calendar to make something there, and a small card opens beside it for a task, a routine or unavailable time.
  • Time tracking is drawn as an orbit — in the top bar and in a floating window that stays above everything else, with its controls appearing only on hover.
  • The planner’s own colour picker, everywhere a colour can be anything.
  • The list view is a table you edit in place, with sortable headings and columns you can size and reorder.
  • The routine window became one short form instead of four tabs.
1.1.0 Sep 21, 2026
  • Backups go to a folder you chose, not Downloads — asked for at the end of setup, and repeatable on a schedule.
  • How tasks work is yours to set. Switch panel sections off, invent your own fields, choose whether subtasks are full tasks, and name your own lanes.
  • Blocked notifications say what to do about it, in plain steps.
  • Five categories to start with instead of ten, and planners on the old ten move across.
  • The foot of the sidebar says who you are, where the planner is kept, and opens Settings.
1.0.0 Sep 9, 2026
  • The first release. A dashboard, a calendar, a task board, an Eisenhower matrix, routines with streaks and notes — on Windows, macOS and Linux, plus a single HTML file that runs from a double-click.
About the numbering. A version number only moves when a release is published, so every number that exists is one you can download and this list reads straight through with no gaps. One exception: the batch first published as 1.2.0 on Sep 21 was rebuilt as 1.1.1 the next day, to keep 1.2.0 for the rebrand.
