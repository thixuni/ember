Personal planner

Your week, in one place.

A dashboard for today, a calendar, a task board, an Eisenhower matrix, routines, notes and time tracking — all sharing one set of categories, so a task you write once shows up wherever you look for it.

Download for your computer View the source

Windows, macOS and Linux. Free, and yours to keep.

Six views, one set of tasks

Categories work like calendar layers — uncheck one and it disappears everywhere at once.

Dashboard

Today on one page — what is due, today’s routines, anything overdue or missed, open action items from your notes, and a scratch pad.

Calendar

Week and month views. Tasks run along the top, routines sit in the time grid, and a Catch-up panel gathers anything overdue.

Tasks

A board you drag cards across — Backlog through Dropped — or the same tasks as a list grouped by month, with quick and advanced filters.

Matrix

Every task lands in an Eisenhower quadrant from whether it is urgent, important, both or neither — closest due date first.

Routines

Anything that repeats: every day, weekdays, chosen days, or every few days. Check off the day squares to keep a streak going: any day counts, and doing it on a day off makes up for a missed one.

Notes

Write freely, tag by category, and turn any line into an action item — it becomes a real task on the board and the calendar.

Time

Where the hours actually went, broken down by day, by category and by task, with what you estimated against what it took.

Open a task, see everything about it

A panel slides in from the right — no full-screen detour, no losing your place.

Details and history

Dates, priority, tags, linked tasks, files, an estimate and subtasks on one tab. Every change the task has been through on the other.

Time, against an estimate

Give a task an estimate and press Start. The timer counts up against it and turns red past it, so overtime shows rather than hides. A small always-on-top window stays above whatever you are working in, and every run lands on the calendar at the hour it happened.

Comments and documents

Talk to yourself about a task as you go. Documents are markdown, and point the app at an Obsidian vault to have them mirrored there — edits on either side find their way across.

Google Calendar, both ways

Your Google events show on the calendar and dashboard. Dated tasks and routines go into your Google calendar, and a change made on either side finds its way to the other.

Three of these need the installed app. The floating timer, the Obsidian vault sync and Google Calendar work on the desktop version, because a browser tab cannot float above other apps, reach a folder on your disk, or keep a Google sign-in safe.

Just the one file

A single HTML file that runs in any browser by double-clicking it. No install, no updates.

Download the .html

A warning you can expect. The installers are not code signed yet, so Windows may show a blue “Windows protected your PC” box — choose More info, then Run anyway. On macOS, right-click the app and choose Open the first time.

Your planner stays on your computer

There is no account, no server and no sync. Everything you type is stored by the app on your own machine and never leaves it — which also means clearing your browser data can erase it, so use File › Back up planner to save a copy or move it to another computer.
